## Key Testimony  
### Administrative Documents on Sobibor  
- Prosecution sought to show that SS-Obersturmführer Wirth was an RSHA (Reichssicherheitshauptamt) officer and one of Sobibor’s commanders.  
- Documents tendered:  
  • Polish report on Sobibor (with Hebrew translation) – marked ת/1293.  
  • Notification of Wirth’s promotion (19 Aug 1943) – marked ת/1294.  
  • April 19, 1943 letter concerning camp command – marked ת/1296.  
- Discussion clarified that camp policing and executions were carried out by RSHA personnel, not the economic-administrative offices.  

### Podchlebnik’s Account of Chelmno  
- Deported from Kolo in late 1941 by SS and German police to Chelmno, under pretext of labor.  
- Held in a cellar (“the mortar room”), saw inscriptions on walls:  
  • “No one leaves here alive.”  
  • “Every day/night 2–3 of us go out and never return.”  
  • Warnings about shooting those unfit for work.  
- Heard German announcements inviting prisoners to “the bathhouse” before gassing.  
- Witnessed sealed vans used to kill victims with exhaust gas.  
- Ukrainians and a German would remove gold teeth and rings from corpses—sometimes cutting off fingers to extract rings.  
- Forced to bury bodies and sort victims’ personal effects (shoes, clothing) into layered pits.  
- Account of work details in a nearby forest: digging mass-grave pits, escorting victims.  
- Personal tragedy: wife and two children among those killed in gas vans; he begged SS to shoot him but was beaten and forced to work.  
- Suicide attempt by hanging thwarted by fellow inmates.  
- Escape plan: with four others, requested cigarettes to distract SS guards, cut through truck canvas, jumped off a moving vehicle en route to the mass-grave site, and fled into the forest.  
- Hid for 48 hours on a farmer’s snowbound loft near Chelmno, then reunited briefly with fellow escapee.  
- Later transported by train in “death wagons”—carriage inscriptions read “Heading toward death” (Yiddish and Polish).  
- Fled through the Lodz area in 1944, evaded SS patrol, crossed roads, and ultimately survived.
