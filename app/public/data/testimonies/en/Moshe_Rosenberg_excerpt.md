Moshe Rosenberg, a Hungarian engineer and prominent Jewish public figure, was the Chairman of the Jewish National Fund and a member of Budapest’s Relief and Rescue Committee from 1942 until June 1944. He testified to coordinating aid to Jews in provincial towns, channeling funds and dispatching able-bodied youths to obtain forged papers and urge their kin to flee the ghettos and seek the Aryan side, acts carried out with the resources of Kasztner and the Rescue Committee. His testimony highlighted the fragile, improvised networks of rescue under occupation, the grim calculus of trying to save lives against a rapidly closing window, and the historical importance of documenting such efforts as evidence of Jewish resistance and the limits of human agency within the Holocaust.
