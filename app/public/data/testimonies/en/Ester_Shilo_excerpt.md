Esther Shiloh, born in 1925 in Łódź, was a Lodz-born survivor who endured the ghetto and the deportations to Auschwitz, later living in Givatayim. As a teenager and a member of a Zionist youth movement, she helped organize a memorial meeting for Bialik and Herzl in 1944, wearing the yellow badge and serving as one of the group’s chief spokesmen, urging her companions to lift their heads and smile despite hunger, cold, and degradation. Her testimony, preserved in the Eichmann Trial records, captures a moment of quiet resistance and cultural memory—demonstrating how dignity, memory, and communal morale endured under Nazi oppression and shaping the historical record of the Holocaust.
