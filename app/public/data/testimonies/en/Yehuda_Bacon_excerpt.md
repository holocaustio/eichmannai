Yehuda Bakon, a fourteen-year-old artist from Moravska Ostrava, testified as a survivor deported first to Theresienstadt and then to Auschwitz in December 1943, where he became the sole survivor of his family. He recalled the abrupt, dehumanizing arrival—being stripped, compelled to walk naked into a “sauna” for disinfection, and signing declarations that he arrived as a Schutzhaeftling “of his own free will” to shield him from the fury of the people. In the Familien-Lager for children, organized by Fredy Hirsch, he found a brief span of schooling and routine, even as a card marked SB nach 6 Monaten spelled out the grim promise of six months before “special treatment” and the gas chambers, a chilling reminder of how ordinary life was weaponized into extermination and how a young life bore witness to a history of systematic deceit, fragmentation, and murder.
