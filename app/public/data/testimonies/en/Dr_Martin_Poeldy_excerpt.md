Dr. Martin Foeldi, a lawyer by training who practiced in Uzhgorod in Carpatho-Russia and later sat on the Ghetto Committee established by the town’s Jewish leadership, described the rapid handover of authority to German-backed officials after the occupation. He recounted the ghetto’s creation in a brick factory on the town’s outskirts, housing about 14,000 Jews in a space barely fit for 2,000, with horrific sanitation—open latrines and near-constant overcrowding that demoralized inmates from the outset—and the exaction of enormous fines such as 1.5 million pengoe demanded by the Gestapo, with his own family paying 100,000 pengoe. He recalled how a Gestapo officer sought to mislead the Jews with assurances of a normal life in Germany, and how the Gestapo maintained constant contact with the local Council, illustrating the bureaucratic machinery that translated persecution into deportation and mass murder. This testimony, part of the Eichmann trial record, underscores the collaboration between local authorities and Nazi security that enabled the Holocaust to unfold in occupied territories.
