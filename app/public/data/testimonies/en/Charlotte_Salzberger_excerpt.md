Charlotte Salzberger, born Charlotte Sidonie Wreschner in 1923 in Frankfurt, testified about her family’s flight to the Netherlands in 1934 and their subsequent life there as stateless German Jews. She recalled the imposition of Jewish registration and the yellow star, and that in October 1942 she and her sister received a summons to report for Arbeitseinsatz which they refused to obey, a choice set within a spectrum of responses described by survivors—compliance, fatalistic flight, and deferral through seeking foreign citizenship—culminating in Ecuadorian passports and a Stockholm visa as a fragile lifeline amid Nazi deportations.
