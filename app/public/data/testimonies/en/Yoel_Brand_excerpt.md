Joel Brand, a German-raised Polish Jew who settled in Budapest and became a leading Zionist organizer, served as a vice-president of the Budapest Palestine Office and as head of the Vaadat Tiyul, the touring rescue committee that coordinated cross-border relief efforts. He recounted how, in 1941, tens of thousands of Jews were suddenly rounded up and deported to Poland, and how his own sister-in-law and her husband were seized; after pleading with them in a transit camp, he enlisted the help of a Hungarian counter-espionage officer, Joszi Krem, paying 10,000 pengoes to recover them and learning for the first time of mass shootings. Brand described the Relief and Rescue Committee—led by Rezsoe Kasztner with Samu Springmann and himself directing rescue work across borders—uncovering how underground networks tried to move Jews to safety and how these negotiations with Hungarian and Nazi authorities embodied a perilous, morally fraught bid to preserve life amid the orbit of Nazi genocide.
