Israel Carmel, a Tel Aviv magistrate and former Israeli consul in Poland, testified that he spent months reading Hans Frank’s diary and assembling extracts specifically about the Jewish problem in the General Government. He explained that the material consisted largely of meetings, reports, and day‑to‑day records—often showing the Gestapo as the driving force rather than articulating independent Jewish policy decisions—and highlighted passages on the 1939–1940 resettlement and deportation programs. By translating the Hebrew edition and submitting the photocopies to the court, Carmel supplied the Eichmann trial with a painstaking documentary bridge between bureaucratic minutes and the execution of genocide, underscoring how archival evidence can anchor historical accountability for the Holocaust.
