Avraham Hagag, an officer in the Criminal Identification Department - Investigation Division at the National Headquarters of the Israel Police, testified as a fluent Arabic reader who examined a photocopy of a 1944 Turkish diary (an Agenda) in which Latin names appear beside Arabic remarks, including Eichmann. He described deciphering the Arabic lines above Eichmann’s name as “fairus nadira jiddan” and “Wa-Kheir Mukhlis lil-Arab”—“a very rare diamond” and “the best redeemer for the Arabs”—and stated that the handwriting matched the distinctive style of Hajj Amin al-Husseini, not Farouk, noting the Mufti’s ornamental flourishes. The testimony provided a rare documentary link between Nazi figures and Arab leadership, illustrating the transnational reach of collaboration and propaganda during the war and underscoring the meticulous forensic work that lent historical weight to the Eichmann Trial.
