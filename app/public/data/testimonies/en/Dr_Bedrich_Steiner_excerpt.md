Dr. Bedrich Steiner, a Prague-trained lawyer who returned to Slovakia in 1939 with Gestapo permission and later headed the statistical department of the Jewish Centre, testified about the bureaucratic machinery by which Jews were registered for emigration and, after the war, about the archival work that made their persecution legible. He detailed the 1942 deportations—57,837 Jews in 57 transports, including 18,746 to Auschwitz, 4,501 to Lublin, and 34,590 to the Opole area—numbers he was able to verify only after the war, when he assisted in reconstructing the records. In the postwar period, Steiner led a documentation operation in Pressburg for the Zionist Organization and the Union of Jewish Communities, obtaining and photographing documents from state archives and the Vasek trial, thereby supplying crucial evidence of Slovakia’s role in the Final Solution and helping to anchor the historical record of the Holocaust.
