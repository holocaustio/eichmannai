Mordechai Ansbacher, born January 11, 1927, in Würzburg, was a German-born Jewish survivor who later worked at Yad Vashem in Jerusalem. He recounted how, after the November 1938 pogrom, he was sent in 1939 to Belgium with a transport of Jewish children, lived in Brussels, and, during the occupation, attempted to flee to England only to be recaptured and held in a small concentration camp near Calais before being sent back to Belgium and ultimately repatriated to Würzburg in January 1941 thanks to his parents’ intervention; his brother remained in Gestapo custody at Neuendorf and was later sent to Auschwitz. His testimony illuminates the fragile networks of refuge, the bureaucratic machinery of deportation, and the intimate tragedy of families under Nazi rule, providing a human, firsthand thread through which the Eichmann Trial could trace the larger history of persecution and displacement across occupied Europe.
