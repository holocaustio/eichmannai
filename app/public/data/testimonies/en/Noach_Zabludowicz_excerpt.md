Noach Zabludowicz, born in 1919 in Ciechanow and later employed by the Electricity Corporation in Tel Aviv, testified about his experiences as a Jew under German occupation in 1940–41. He recollected the Nasielsk station scene when a German stationmaster in a brown SS uniform ordered all Jews off the train; when none moved, he shot a baby and then a woman who shrieked, before commanding everyone to reboard. His broader account of anti-Jewish decrees, forced labor, confiscations, and the tightening ghetto laid bare the bureaucratic brutality of the Nazi regime and the intimate, human cost of terror witnessed in ordinary towns, underscoring the Eichmann Trial’s aim to document the machinery of genocide.
