Zyndel Shmuel Grynszpan, born in 1886 in Radomsko, Poland, had lived in Germany since 1911 and settled in Hanover with his wife, raising eight children—including two sons and a daughter. On 27 October 1938, police arrested the entire family, forced them to sign deportation orders at the Revier, and herded them with about six hundred others to the Concert House by the River Leine before loading them onto trains toward the German-Polish border as crowds cried, “Out with the Jews! To go Palestine!,” a mass expulsion that encompassed roughly twelve thousand people from across the country. Grynszpan testified that in 1938 he had two sons and a daughter still alive; one son survived and was present in this country, while the other was killed by the Germans, and the eldest child was a daughter, underscoring the intimate, devastating toll of the Polenaktion and marking a stark prelude to the broader catastrophe of the Holocaust.
