Yitzhak Nehama, born in 1910 in Salonika, served as a male nurse in a Greek military hospital in Korytsa and fought at the Tepelene front in Albania before the German advance forced his return, marching 28 days home to Salonika under occupation. Upon his return he witnessed the rapid, systematic dispossession of Jewish life: radios had to be surrendered within 24 hours on pain of execution, pianos and telephones were taken, and three trucks removed the community’s library of holy books, provoking the rabbis to tears as they cried, “Nothing matters to us, only these books.” He recalled the unmistakable presence of brown-uniformed Gestapo figures and, in the lead‑up to deportation, the 11 July 1942 German-language newspaper Das neue Europa announcing that Jews aged 18–45 must report the next day at Liberation Square, a chilling marker of how ordinary daily life was weaponized toward annihilation.
