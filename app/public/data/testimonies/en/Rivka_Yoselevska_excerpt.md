Rivka Yoselewska, a survivor from Powost-Zagorodski who later resided in Ramat Gan and came from a family of artisans (her father ran a leather goods shop), testified about the 1941 German invasion and the rapid destruction of her town’s Jewish life. She recalled how the synagogue was turned into a stable, and how the town’s Jews—about 150, including the rabbi and the ritual slaughterer—were assembled, forced to wear the rabbi’s prayer shawl and address the crowd, beaten, and driven to a shallow ditch at the old cemetery to be shot, as frightened villagers watched and later told the tale. Her account also conveyed intimate acts of courage and suffering—the grandfather who refused to flee, the mother who cried out to protect her children, and a girl who hid sugar to feed the others—illustrating the human dimension of annihilation and providing a crucial, documentary witness to the scale and religious dimension of Nazi violence that the Eichmann trial sought to record.
