Zvi Henryk Zimmerman, a Polish Jewish lawyer who later became a member of the Knesset, testified that he remained in Cracow during the war and lived in the Cracow Ghetto until March 1943, taking a leadership role in an underground Zionist network. Within Ha-Sneh, a roof organization uniting Zionist youth and Jewish intelligentsia, he served as a liaison with the Fighting Halutz in the Ghetto and helped organize hideouts, arms caches, and false papers while maintaining contact with the outside world. He recalled that refugees from Germany included infiltrators—non-Jews who claimed knowledge of Jewish life, some of whom proved to be Nazis—and spoke of rumors of a centralized department handling expulsions, headed by an expert versed in Hebrew and Yiddish, with ties to Palestine, and of the March 1942 Aktion that foreshadowed the broader machinery of deportation and extermination in Cracow.
