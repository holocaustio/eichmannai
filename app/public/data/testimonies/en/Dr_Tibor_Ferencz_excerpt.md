Dr. Tibor Ferencz was a Hungarian advocate who, after the war, served as a prosecutor with the General People’s Prosecution Office in Budapest and rose to Deputy Chief Prosecutor, directing the prosecutions of war criminals. Having learned that his wife and mother-in-law had been sent to Auschwitz and that his home had been looted, he devoted himself to the service of the Jewish people and to bringing perpetrators to justice. He personally directed trials and, in the case of Endre and Baky, visited their death cells hours before execution to question them separately and ensured they were hanged on the same day after clemency was denied, a stark testament to the immediacy and moral weight of postwar accountability in Hungary.
