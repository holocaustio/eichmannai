Zivia Lubetkin-Zuckerman, a Warsaw-based Zionist activist, member of Kibbutz Lohamei Ha-Gettaot and wife of Yitzhak Zuckerman, testified about the early German occupation of Warsaw and the rapid imposition of restrictions on Jews. She recounted how prohibitions proliferated—from wearing the Shield of David to bans on currency, trade, medical aid for Jews, and even movement—creating a sense that Jews were beyond the rule of law, and she bore witness to the terror of deterrence, recalling the Nalewski 9 courtyard where fifty Jews were seized in the morning and never returned after a Polish policeman’s death was avenged. Lubetkin also described the systematic removal of Jewish intelligentsia and the suppression of cultural life, underscoring how civilians were deprived of legal protections and human dignity, an eyewitness account essential for understanding the Nazi project as it took hold in Polish cities.
