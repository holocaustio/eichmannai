Dr. Aharon Beilin, a physician who later lived in Tel Aviv, testified that in February 1943 he was deported from Bialystok to Auschwitz with about five thousand Jews, traveling in two trains, and that he and his mother were caught while his wife sought shelter in another bunker. He recalled the brutal selection by SS doctor Dr. Rohde, aided by a dog, with the aria La Donna e Mobile echoing as roughly 150 men and 150 women were chosen for removal while the rest were sent toward the crematorium, and he saw trucks return bearing his mother’s coat, a devastating sign that she had perished. Beilin also recounted his mother’s wish that, if he survived, he would go to Palestine, a vow he kept, and his testimony stands as a stark record of the organized machinery of annihilation—the barbed wire, the quarantine block, and the RSHA designation—a human witness to the genocide that the Eichmann trial sought to preserve for history.
