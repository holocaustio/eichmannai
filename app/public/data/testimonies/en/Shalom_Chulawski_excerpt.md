Shalom Cholawski, a Belorussian Jew and later a member of Kibbutz Ein Hashofet, testified about the Nesvizh ghetto uprising of 1942 and his subsequent escape into the forest. He recalled the ghetto’s collapse under SS and Lithuanian assault, the residents’ refusal to submit to a selection, the ghetto being set alight, and the formation of a Jewish fighting detachment that protected survivors and established family camps in the woods. He also described the January 1943 liberation of the Sverze forced-labor camp, where with partisan Posisodski he freed two hundred and fifty Jews, a striking example of organized resistance that illuminates the wider, perilous struggle of Jewish partisans in Belorussia and Ukraine during the Holocaust.
