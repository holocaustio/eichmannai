Joseph Zalman Kleinman, born January 30, 1930, Jerusalem resident and Holocaust survivor, testified about being deported from Zeliezovce to Auschwitz at age fourteen with his parents and sister, only to be separated at the station and learn that his family had been sent to the gas chambers. In Birkenau, he was confined in Camp D among older inmates and observed the removal of youths under sixteen to labor camps, while secretly approaching the nearby Sonderkommando—Jews who worked in the crematoria—for occasional food and quiet dialogue, and he recalled witnessing a fourteen-year-old flogging that ended with an unbroken, silent endurance that haunted him later. Kleinman’s testimony underscored the systematic targeting of youth, the existence and function of the crematoria and gas chambers at Birkenau, and the broader mechanism of annihilation that the Eichmann trial sought to record and indict.
