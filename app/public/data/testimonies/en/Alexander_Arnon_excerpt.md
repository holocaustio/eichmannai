Alexander Arnon, sixty-three at the time, had been the secretary of the Jewish Community in Zagreb and later directed HICEM, the emigration organization, as well as JOINT’s territorial operations and the territorial committee for aid to refugees. In April 1941, as German troops occupied Zagreb and the Independent State of Croatia was proclaimed, the Gestapo arrived at the Jewish Community offices on the eve of Passover, confiscated the building and its funds—about 700,000 dinars—sealed the cashbox with a red stamp reading “Gestapo—Geheime Staatspolizei,” and launched hours-long interrogations about the archives and the Community’s activities, even as anti-Jewish notices appeared in shops. Arnon’s testimony illuminates the rapid collapse of communal life under a puppet regime and the decisive role of Croatian fascists, the Ustaše, in the early acts of persecution, marking a pivotal moment in the Holocaust in Yugoslavia.
