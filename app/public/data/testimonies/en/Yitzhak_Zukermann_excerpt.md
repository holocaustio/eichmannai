Yitzhak Zuckerman, the husband of Zivia Lubetkin and a participant in the wartime underground who would later live at Kibbutz Lohamei Ha-Gettaot, testified about his experiences after the German occupation when he and several hundred Jews were transported from Warsaw to the Kampinos labor camp on the last day of Passover in 1941. He described ten-to-twelve hour days of work—often up to their necks in water—beneath meager rations, where death by starvation and the constant threat of execution by a camp leadership and Gestapo guard force overshadowed every moment; the camp’s guards wore a mosaic of uniforms, from black Gestapo to the brown-collared SD and Waffen-SS, and interrogations were brutal punishments. His escape, facilitated by an underground network and a Polish liaison, Lonka Pozhivieska, after a brutal summons and beating, underscored the persistence of resistance even as the system sought to exterminate; the testimony thus offers a harrowing, first-hand portrait of Kampinos and anchors the Eichmann trial in the lived brutality of Nazi labor camps and the courageous networks that endured against it.
