Avraham Gordon, born in Budapest in 1927, was a Jewish pupil in the sixth grade who witnessed the German occupation of Hungary in March 1944 and the rapid closure of schools, followed by the imposition of the yellow Shield of David around 5 April. He described being conscripted with hundreds of Jewish youths for labor at the Schwabenberg, under Jewish engineer Kolbach and SS liaison Buehring, where they drilled two tunnels toward the Eden and Majestic Hotels and performed outside tasks, all amid a climate of coercion and fear. In mid-May, Gordon and about fifteen others—ten young people and five adults—were transferred to Rose Hill, the private villa of Leopold Aschner that would house Adolf Eichmann, where a German named Slawik supervised the digging of ditches in the garden; the episode highlighted the intimate machinery of the Holocaust in wartime Hungary, exposing how ordinary youths were drawn into the regime’s infrastructure of terror and the complicity that enabled Eichmann’s operations to unfold on Hungarian soil.
