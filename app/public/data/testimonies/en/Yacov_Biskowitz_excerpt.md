Ya'akov Biskowitz, fifteen and a half at the time, testified as a constable at the National Headquarters of the Israel Police who was taken with his father, a carpenter, from Hrubieszow to Sobibor in June 1942. He described the arrival and selection—carpenters singled out, women driven to Camp 3 and men to the left—followed by the murder of the chosen, first in a Lazarette and later in Camp 3 where eighty men burned the corpses, while he himself worked in Camp 1 among artisans such as shoemakers, carpenters, tailors, and a goldsmith. He also helped prepare a sketch of the camp, illustrating the layout and the operations that included gas chambers and the pigsty-like Camp 2, a testimony that offers a stark, granular record of Sobibor’s machinery of murder and, in turn, a crucial piece of the historical record for the Eichmann trial.
