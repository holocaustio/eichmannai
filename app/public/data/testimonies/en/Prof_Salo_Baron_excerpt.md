Shalom Baron, known in English as Salo Wittmayer Baron, was a professor of Jewish History at Columbia University and the author of monumental works on Jewish communities, including The Religious and Social History of the Jewish People. He presented himself as a historian rather than an eyewitness or jurist, offering two apologies for his Hebrew and for the double challenge of interpreting contemporary history without a long retrospective while drawing on a vast body of sources—from German archives to diaries and autobiographies—that were becoming available to illuminate the prewar world. He depicted the 1930s as a period of unprecedented crisis for Europe and for the Jewish people, yet testified to their enduring vitality and creative power, arguing that the immense documentary record—though born of persecution—made possible a historical understanding essential to grasping how the Holocaust unfolded.
