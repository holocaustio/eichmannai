Avraham Levinson, born in Lublin and later a Tel Aviv merchant, testified as a survivor who endured the Belzec labour camp and the liquidation of the Lublin Ghetto. He described a brutal winter morning—the Day of Atonement—when Jewish prisoners of war from the Polish army, many barefoot and in torn uniforms, were marched out under SS guards, and the next day witnessed two Jewish community orderlies in German uniforms loading the living and the dead onto carts, delivering them beyond the town; years later he learned those POWs were transported to Majdanek’s Field No. 6 and burned. He also noted the Judenkarte and the term Selbstschutz, illustrating how certificates and local collaborators facilitated, or at least attempted to regulate, survival amid early acts of mass murder that culminated in the Majdanek atrocities, providing a stark, on-the-ground account of the mechanisms of liquidation that shaped the Holocaust and informed the Eichmann trial’s evidence.
