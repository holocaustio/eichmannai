Dr. Ya'akov Hugo Kratky, a physician from Maehrisch-Ostrau, was one of seventeen doctors aboard a thousand-strong transport in October 1939 sent toward Galicia as part of the initial Nisko deportation plan. He recalled marching from the railway to a hilltop camp where an SS officer declared in German, “Sie bauen hier ein Lager… Wasser hier vergiftet mit Typhus. Wenn Ihr Wasser trinken wollt, muesst Ihr einen Brunnen graben, aber vom Gipfel, von hier,” the moment in which two huts, barbed wire, and no water were to be established, signaling a prepared concentration site. Kratky’s testimony, including his own choice to drink the water despite official orders and the complicity of doctors in orchestrating mass displacement, illuminated the calculated brutality and bureaucratic coercion at the dawn of the Holocaust, a pivotal eyewitness account of how ordinary medical professionals became tools of genocide.
