The witness was M. Grynszpan, the brother of Hirsch Hermann Grynszpan, who recounted that Hirsch left Hanover for Frankfurt in 1935 to study in a yeshiva and was last seen in 1936 before moving to Paris, where he would remain out of sight for years. He described finding and identifying letters Hirsch had written from Fresnes prison in Paris in 1939, including a March 29 note signed “Hershel” and addressed to Leo in Israel, which bore the handwriting of his brother and spoke of family questions and life in prison. He also recalled attempting to reach Hirsch via the Red Cross in early 1940 after crossing into Russia, receiving only a torn reply, and subsequently halting correspondence to prevent further letters from falling into German hands, a moving testament to a family’s fragile lifeline amid the widening catastrophe.
