David Wdowinski, a professor of psychology and psychiatry at the New School for Social Research in New York and, during the war, the commander of the Irgun Zva'i Leumi in the Warsaw Ghetto, testified about the collapse of Warsaw’s strongholds and the trajectory from uprising to captivity. He recounted the Budzyn episode, where 807 Warsaw Jews were selected by SS men for forced labor in a Heinkel aircraft factory, and he described the brutal acts of commandant Reinhold Feiks, who publicly killed a Jew who refused to undress and later shot an elderly, respected physician, Dr. Pupko. As a camp doctor, Wdowinski tended the living and the dead—overseeing graves, guarding sanitation, and noting the removal of gold teeth before burial—illuminating how the Nazi system combined administrative brutality with the commodification of human life, and marking a crucial, personal witness to the industrialized murder and dehumanization at the heart of the Holocaust.
