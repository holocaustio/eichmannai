Shmuel Horowitz, a tailor by trade who had lived in Kolomea since 1922, testified about being forced to work for the Gestapo under Weissmann after the German occupation. He recounted the first action—the burning of the great synagogue and the rounding up of about two thousand Jews who were loaded into trucks and shot in the nearby forest at Szeparowce—and described the weekly terror that crowded the ghetto’s rooms with thousands, including the moment when a child and a mother were shot as a Schutzpolizei officer pursued them, an event he witnessed in a section where his brother-in-law lived. His testimony also notes the regime’s calculated measures, such as the Jewish Council’s supposed hospital under Kommissar Leideritz, illustrating how the Nazi machinery operated on the ground and marking a crucial, harrowing record of genocide witnessed firsthand for the Eichmann trial.
