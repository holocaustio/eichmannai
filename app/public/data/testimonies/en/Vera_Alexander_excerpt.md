Vera Alexander, a Slovak-born art critic living in Nes Ziona, testified about her 1942 deportation from Slovakia to Auschwitz under the Hlinka Guard, after being seized in early April and transported by freight car. She recalled the shattering awakening upon arrival, when the propaganda she had read proved false—the shaven heads, lice-infested uniforms, and the brutal routine in blocks 7 and 9, where she and others endured back-breaking work in the Landwirtschafts-kommando and the senseless tasks that filled the day, often under the constant threat of death for the slightest misstep. As Blockaelteste in the quarantine Block 3, she navigated a perilous line between obedience and humanity, managing to ease some misery by allowing quarantined women to sit on their beds and shielding others when SS officers approached, a testament to small acts of resistance that, she claimed, saved lives and exposed the moral calculus at the core of Auschwitz, a grim witness to the daily functioning of totalitarian terror documented for the Eichmann trial.
