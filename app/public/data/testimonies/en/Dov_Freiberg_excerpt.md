Dov Freiberg, a Ramle resident and former production inspector at a motor-engine factory, survived the Lodz and Warsaw ghettos before fleeing to the Lublin district and being transported to Sobibor in May 1942. He recalled the airless, crowded freight-car ride during which two women died, the rapid SS and Ukrainian guards’ orders of “Raus, raus” and “Links, rechts” that split men from women and children and delivered them to the camp’s gas chambers, while the men were kept in the yard at night. Freiberg also spoke of the gate marked “SS Sonderkommando Umsiedlungslager—Camp for Resettlement,” the band at Lager 1, and the pervasive disbelief among victims that extermination could occur, a testimony to the once-imperceptible, industrial scale of murder that underpinned the Holocaust and a crucial eyewitness record of Sobibor’s early operations.
