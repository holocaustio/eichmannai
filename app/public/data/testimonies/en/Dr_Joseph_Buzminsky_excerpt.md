Dr. Josef Buzminsky, a physician from Przemyśl who survived thanks to his Polish wife who hid him with fifteen others, testified about life in the ghetto after autumn 1941, describing twelve-hour days of hard labor under the H. V. administration and brutal deprivations of food and medicine. He recalled the Ordnungsdienst, the spread of disease such as typhus, and the two deportation operations to Belzec, where residents were forced to congregate at the Umschlagplatz, shown “stamps of life” to mark who could work and who would be sent away, under the pretext of relocation. Among the most poignant moments were the public murder of Dr. Grebstein, who was beaten to death after displaying his diplomas, and the deaths of his brother Isidor and his sister, scenes that underscored how a bureaucratic system transformed orders into extermination and left a stark, indelible record of the Holocaust’s mechanized brutality.
