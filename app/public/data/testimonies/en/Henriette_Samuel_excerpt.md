Henriette Samuel, born in Berlin, testified as the widow of Oslo’s Rabbi Samuel, who had been called to Norway in 1930 and served as Chief Rabbi of the country. She recalled a once-thriving Oslo Jewish community of about 1,200 and a gradual tightening of oppression—identity cards stamped “Jude,” radios confiscated, and increasing Gestapo scrutiny—culminating on 2 September 1942 when her husband and twelve men from Nersnes were taken to Grini and never returned, leaving her to raise three children and endure the unknown fate of her partner. Her account underscores the erasure of Norway’s Jewish leadership and the quiet courage of the underground, exemplified by neighbors like Ingebjorg Sletten Fosstvet, and situates this personal tragedy within a broader historical arc—from a country of humanitarian ideals to a site of Nazi persecution.
