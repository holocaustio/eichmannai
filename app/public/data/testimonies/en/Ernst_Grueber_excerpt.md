Heinrich Karl Ernst Grueber was a Berlin Protestant pastor who, after 1945, served as Probst (dean) in Berlin; before the war he ministered in Karlsdorf, an eastern suburb of the city. In his testimony he recalled his close dealings with the Reich Representation and Reich Association of German Jews—Chief Rabbi Leo Baeck, Dr. Otto Hirsch, and Mr. Eppstein—and described using his government contacts to aid his Jewish friends: negotiating on matters of emigration, legal protection, and urgent relief, including arranging a pre-Pesach 1940 import of wheat flour so Jews could bake matzos. The recollection of standing with Jewish leaders who could not publicly speak, and of Baeck’s Knebelbart and the pre-festival dread, underscored the fragile humanity that persisted within an oppressive bureaucracy, marking Grueber’s testimony as a crucial thread in the documentary record of civilian solidarities that sought to mitigate persecution.
