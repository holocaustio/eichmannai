Yisrael Gutman, a member of the Warsaw Ghetto fighting organization who later joined Kibbutz Lahavot Habashan in the Upper Galilee, testified as a survivor of the Warsaw Uprising and its aftermath. He recounted being wounded in a bunker, exposed to poison, and transported in an overcrowded freight car to Majdanek, where SS guards lined prisoners up in rows of five and drove them toward the camp. There, in Feld No. 4—the worst field—he described bunk-huts housing hundreds, 4:30 roll-calls, the bathhouse where hair was shaved, and the relentless, ritualized selections that revealed the cold efficiency of Nazi extermination; Gutman’s testimony thus preserves a stark, indispensable eyewitness account of the Holocaust for the Eichmann trial.
