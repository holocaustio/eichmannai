Michael Podchlewnik, a chocolate-factory worker from Kolo who later lived in Benei Berak, testified that at the war’s outset he was deported with about thirty others to Chelmno in late 1941. He described being herded into a palace-turned-prison, locked in a cellar where inscriptions warned “No one comes out of here alive,” and the stark routine of people being loaded into gas vans, the screams of those inside, and the subsequent gathering of their shoes as witnesses returned to the scene. His account offered a chilling, firsthand portrait of Chelmno’s extermination methods—the use of gas vans, mass murder, and the lingering evidentiary traces—that reinforced the Eichmann trial’s historic record and the broader understanding of the Nazi genocide.
