Perla Mark, a Polish-born Jew who had studied pharmacology at the University of Czernowitz and was married to Abraham Jakob Mark, the city’s Chief Rabbi, testified about the cataclysm that overtook Czernowitz in July 1941. On 7 July 1941, four SS men and two armed guards burst into her home, pressed her husband for 1,000 trucks, and, after he refused to name people with jewellery, abducted him and carried him to the synagogue and then to the Schwarzer Adler Hotel cellar, where he was held for a day as a neighbour’s husband was beaten for failing to polish the attackers’ shoes; the next day they brought petrol and oil into the synagogue, set it aflame, threw the sixty Scrolls of the Law into the flames, and led her husband up to the roof to show him the burning building. The following day, some 150–160 Jews from the Cultural Centre and the Schwarzer Adler were taken from the cellar to the Prut river and shot; Mark learned of her husband’s fate through conversations with others, including an Archbishop’s representative who said that “If the Jews had not shot at the Germans, the Germans would not have murdered the Jews,” and a university professor who lamented the Germans’ arrogance; within a month the remaining Jews were confined to a ghetto, marking the destruction of Czernowitz’s once-thriving Jewish community.
