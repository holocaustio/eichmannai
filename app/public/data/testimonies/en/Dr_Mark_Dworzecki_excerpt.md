Dr. Meir Mark Dworzecki, a physician who worked at Kupat Holim in Tel Aviv and later lectured on the Holocaust at Bar-Ilan University, had fought in the 1939 Polish campaign and survived captivity before settling in Israel. In Vilna during the 1941 German occupation, he witnessed the rapid disappearances of Jews, the misleading assurances that they were being taken to a labour camp at Ponar, and the brutally real mass murders in the Ponar forest, including the harrowing testimony of a barefoot woman named Sonia who described how thousands were driven to a clearing, prayers were spoken, and the children’s pleas for water went unanswered as executions began. His eyewitness account, grounded in medical observation and personal memory, underscored the transformation of a beloved landscape into an extermination site and provided a stark, human record that anchored the Eichmann trial and shaped the historical memory of Vilna’s Holocaust.
