The witness was Dr. Sandor Brody, later known as Alexander Brody, a Hungarian-born physician who in 1944 Budapest directed the department for Jewish aid within the Jewish Council (O. M. Z. S. A.). He recalled the German invasion on March 19, 1944, and two waves of arrests—those drawn from a pre-existing hostage list and indiscriminate roundups at railway stations and Danube ports—whose victims were held first at the Rabbinical Academy and on Mosonyi Street, then transported to Kistarcsa, where he was charged with feeding and caring for the detainees. His testimony illuminates the bureaucratic machinery by which Hungarian Jewish leadership sustained detainees under Gestapo control, including travel permits signed by Samu Stern with police and SS oversight and the erasure of signatures that revealed a stark hierarchy making deportations possible, a crucial thread in the Eichmann Trial’s chronicle of Hungary’s role in the Holocaust.
