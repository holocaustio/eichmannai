Adolf Engelstein, born in 1903 in Stonova near Ostrava, was an engineer who directed railway construction in Moravia and later served as an engineer in Theresienstadt’s sanitation and technical departments. In March 1944 he was removed from his post, loaded with huts and building materials onto a transport labeled “Sossen” bound for Germany, and learned only at midnight that he and two colleagues would be sent onward, despite assurances of a “good place of employment.” When the train halted, they faced an SS officer—the Accused—in a private car, a moment that underscored how a bureaucratic machinery of deceit and coercion dragged skilled professionals into deportation, making Engelstein’s testimony a searing, human record of the Holocaust’s organized cruelty.
