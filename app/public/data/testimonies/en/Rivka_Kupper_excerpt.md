Rivka Kuper, née Liebeskind, wife of Adolf Liebeskind—the organizer of the Hebrew underground in Cracow—and a member of Kibbutz Maayan Zvi, testified about the life and peril she endured under Nazi rule. She recalled the staged ghettoization: the marking of apartments, the blue shield of David, the Ausweis system, and the deportations toward the East and to Lublin, culminating in a crowded Podgórze ghetto where disease and hunger spread among the trapped population; she also spoke of the brutalities that accompanied these persecutions, including kidnappings and assaults. Yet she emphasized the underground's tenacity—Hadаrim schooling for children, clandestine worship with Torah scrolls hidden in many houses, and the daily effort to preserve Sabbath and festivals—testifying to a community that forged a lifeline of culture and faith in the face of annihilation, a record of resistance and resilience that illuminates the scale and humanity of what Cracow endured.
