Eliezer Karstadt, a Riga‑based industrialist born in Talsen near Riga, testified about surviving the Nazi occupation in Latvia, remaining in Riga from 1941 until August 1944 before being deported to Stutthof, then Buchenwald, and Bochum, and liberated by American troops on 23 April. He recounted the rapid arrests after the occupation began on 1 July 1941, the disappearance and murder of thousands of Jews, the establishment of the Riga ghetto on 23 October 1941, and the two brutal mass “actions” that followed—on 30 October when a third of the ghetto’s Jews were shot, and in early December when a separate round of killings dragged in craftsmen as well. Karstadt named the Lettish collaborator group Donnerkreuz (Perkonkruzt), led by Herbert Zukos, whom he personally witnessed shooting women and children; survivors described by him, including a woman named Hamburger, confirmed the horror, and he noted the death of the eminent Jewish historian Shim’on Dubnov in the Riga action, all of which underscored the systematic murder, ghettoization, and local complicity that shaped the Holocaust’s reach and provided crucial evidence for the Eichmann trial.
