Shim'on Srebrnik, thirteen years old at the time and a mechanic by trade who later lived in Nes Ziona, testified that in the Lodz ghetto in 1943 his Sabbath walk with his father was interrupted by a shot that felled his father, after which he was seized, loaded onto a truck, and told not to speak. He was taken to Chełmno, where his legs were chained to a chair-like device and remained so until liberation in 1945; there he witnessed the ruin of a villa where Jews had been killed and the scattered bones and remains that the killers left behind. He described the arrival of transports, the gas trucks that killed eighty to a hundred people at a time, the grisly work of the Waldkommando sorting teeth for gold with an Unterscharführer named Walter, and the shooting of men deemed unable to work by Obersturmbannführer Bothmann, details that underscored the industrial scale and intimate cruelty of annihilation and lent stark, irrefutable testimony to the world-at-large during the Eichmann trial.
