Frieda Masia, a nurse who worked in the Jewish hospital in Sosnowiec and later active in the Jewish Community Council set up after the German entry, testified about life under occupation and the Council’s role in administering Jewish life. She recalled the scale and terror of the early years—the Wehrmacht’s roundup of men, the burning of synagogues in Sosnowiec, Katowice, and Bedzin, and Jews being burned alive or shot when they attempted to flee, all alongside a brutal regime of head taxes, gold and silver seizures, and forced labor sent to Germany. Her testimony also highlighted quiet acts of resilience, including an underground school for thousands of Jewish children, demonstrating how culture and education survived under a regime bent on erasing Jewish life, and underscoring the documentary weight of Nazi persecution that the Eichmann trial sought to illuminate.
