Hildegard Henschel, widow of Moritz Henschel—an acclaimed Berlin advocate and notary who headed the Jewish Community’s Department for Homes for the Aged and Sick and served on its presidency—testified about the daily functioning of Jewish life in Berlin under Nazi rule and her role as secretary to the doctors who assessed who could be transported. She recalled the Yom Kippur moment when Gestapo officials summoned community leaders, the announcement of a partial evacuation, and the transformation of Levetzowstrasse synagogue into a Sammellager where the healthy slept on chairs, children occupied separate rooms, and the community maintained kitchens and a first-aid station, even as grim deportations loomed. Her testimony lays bare the coercive machinery of evacuation, the uneasy cooperation of Jewish leadership under persecution, and the shocking chronology of Berlin’s first assembly camps as a crucial thread in the broader tapestry of the Holocaust foregrounded in the Eichmann trial.
