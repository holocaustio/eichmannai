Georges Wellers, a French citizen born in Koslov, was a Maître de Recherches at the National Centre for Scientific Research who directed a physiological-research laboratory at the Sorbonne and had served in the French army in 1939. He described the first major roundups in May 1941—foreign Jews in Paris arrested and sent to Beaune-la-Rolande and Pithiviers—and the second wave in August 1941 that swept thousands from the 11th arrondissement into a transit system culminating at Drancy; he also recalled registering himself as Jewish and omitting his wife, and his arrest on December 12, 1941. The testimony illustrated how bureaucratic measures and brute force converged to deportations, giving historians a concrete portrait of the scale, mechanics, and human vulnerability of the Holocaust in occupied France.
