Dr. Theodor Loewenstein-Lavi, a Romanian-born professor of Jewish history and philosophy who directed the Department for Education and Culture of the Zionist Organization of Romania and led a Jewish secondary school in Bucharest, remained in the capital through 1940–1944. He testified that the German army arrived in Bucharest in late 1940, that by November the Jewish schools were seized and remained under German control, and that a Jewish Centre was established under Lecca, with Richter appearing publicly in 1941. As a scholar who later worked with Yad Vashem, his testimony connected the territorial upheavals—Bessarabia and Bukovina, Transnistria—to the lived experience of Jews in Bucharest, underscoring the systematic destruction of Jewish cultural life and the vital importance of documenting these events for future generations.
