Dr. Franz Eliezer Meyer, born in Breslau and holding a Doctor of Philosophy, had long been active in the Zionist movement and, in Berlin, served as acting chairman of the Zionist Organization and as director of the Palestine Office. In 1933, after Kurt Blumenfeld urged him to relocate, he moved to Berlin to assume leadership, coordinating emigration work and acting as a key liaison within the Reichsvertretung while directing the Palestine Office. His testimony reveals a disciplined, quasi-governmental effort by German Jewry to shelter East European Jews and secure emigration to Palestine under the Nazi regime, illuminating how Zionist leadership and Jewish communal structures operated, even as persecution tightened its grip.
