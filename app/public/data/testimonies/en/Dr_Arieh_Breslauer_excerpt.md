Arye Zvi Breszlauer, a Hungarian-born lawyer who had practiced in Uzhgorod and served as a public functionary in Carpatho-Russia, testified about his experiences in Budapest during the German occupation of 1944 and his attempts to assist Jews in the border regions. Arrested on 23 May and imprisoned for seven weeks, he fell ill with an internal hemorrhage and was kept in hospital without papers as deportations intensified; with the help of Moshe Krausz and the Palestine Office he obtained a certificate and, later, a Swedish Schutzpass through Wallenberg’s network, and was released by the Swiss consulate at the start of August. His account underscored the peril facing Jews in occupied Budapest and the transformative, life-saving force of international rescue efforts—led by Wallenberg and supported by Swedish and Swiss protection—that offered a human counterweight to bureaucratic terror.
