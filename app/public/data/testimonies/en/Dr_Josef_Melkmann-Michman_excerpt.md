Dr. Joseph Melkman, a Dutch-born educator who taught Latin and Greek in Amsterdam, editor of the Zionist weekly, and later director of Yad Vashem from 1957 to 1960, testified about the Nazis’ early grip on the Netherlands. He recounted the 1940 dismissal of Jewish civil servants under an Aryan declaration and, on 22 February 1941, the Sabbath-day round-up of more than 400 young Jews in Amsterdam by the Green Police, who beat them and marched them to camps at Schoorl, then to Buchenwald and Mauthausen, where the Germans warned that no one would emerge alive. Only one of those seized survived, a stark testament to the calculated terror that linked Amsterdam to the broader machinery of deportation, a history Melkman later helped illuminate through his work at Yad Vashem.
