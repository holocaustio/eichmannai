Hela Batsheva Rufeisen, a member of the Bustan Hagalil settlement and a courier for Cracow’s Zionist youth underground, testified that she acted as the liaison between Cracow and the Warsaw underground, ferrying arms, documents, and forged papers to sustain resistance across occupied Poland. She recounted harrowing months when the ghetto siege left her group without a roof or food, sheltering in cellars, rescuing a totally paralysed hospital patient, and continuing to move explosives for the attack on Cafe Cyganeria while coordinating refuges and communications from Cracow to Warsaw and beyond to places such as Rzeszow, Sanok, and Lvov. Her arrest near the Warsaw ghetto, the frantic effort to smuggle money to rescue comrades, and the dramatic moment she discarded an address by throwing it into an oven during a police search underscored the peril, improvisation, and unwavering resolve of Jewish resistance, underscoring the organizational scale and human stakes of underground operations that the Eichmann trial sought to illuminate.
