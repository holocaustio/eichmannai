Mordechai Zurawski, a Jewish butcher living in Gedera, testified that in 1944 he was deported from Łódź and spent seven to eight months at Chelmno, where he was among six strong men separated from his group to work in the camp. He described the terror of marching in four rows with chains on their legs, the deceptive promises of “Leipzig” and baths from men in white coats, and his duties first in the Waldkommando—cutting timber for the crematoria—and later in the Sonderkommando, where he witnessed corpses dragged from gas vans and, at times, a driver shoot a still-living victim. Zurawski stated that during his stay three transports of about a thousand people each arrived, with thousands more having been murdered earlier, and that in September–October 1944 the SS liquidated the camp, dismantling barracks, crematoria, and signs to erase all traces, a destruction that confirmed Chelmno as an early, mechanized site of genocide and the intimate, overwhelming horror endured by those who witnessed and endured it.
