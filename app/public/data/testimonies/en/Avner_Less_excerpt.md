Avner Less, an officer in the Israel Police and a member of the National Headquarters’ Economic Department who was seconded to Bureau 06, acted as the official recorder of the Accused’s statement. He recounted how the Accused was cautioned in German, spoke at length in a taped session, and later reviewed and corrected a written transcript that the Accused signed on each page, with Less preserving the original and its copy for official processing. Less’s careful handling—tape recording, meticulous transcription, and authentication of the final text—illustrated the Eichmann trial’s method of turning the Accused’s own words into a durable, verifiable historical record, and underscored the broader historical importance of documenting the mechanisms of the Nazi regime for posterity and accountability.
