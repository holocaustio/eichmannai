Max Burger, born in Moravska Ostrava and a voluntary worker in the town’s Jewish Community, had been an insurance broker before the war. He recounted how the occupation began with arrests and public restrictions, followed by the burning of synagogues, the registration of Jews under the Gestapo, and the confiscation of belongings, as local leadership complied with orders from Berlin and the Eichmann apparatus; he and others were pressed to prepare for the deportation of 1,000 Jews to a camp in Poland, under the watchful eye of Guenther and the Community leadership. His testimony details the chilling sequence—the circulars, the “Assentierung” at the riding school, Aryan medical examinations, and the sealing of trains under SD guard—that turned bureaucratic routine into mass expulsion, illustrating how the Nazi state extended its reach from Vienna to Moravska Ostrava and forged the machinery of the Final Solution.
