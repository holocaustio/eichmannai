Werner David Melchior, a Danish-born Jew and the son of Denmark’s Chief Rabbi (his father had become acting Chief Rabbi in 1943 after the arrest of Rabbi Friediger), testified about the wartime experience of Danish Jewry and his family’s role within it. He recalled that, until 1943, Jews in Denmark lived on an equal footing with their neighbors, but the Nazi occupation unleashed a wave of anti-Semitic propaganda, including a Danish edition of the Stuermer, and instances of violence against synagogues, accompanied by legal reprisals for those who propagated libel against Jewish figures—even as some Danish authorities initially maintained a modus vivendi with the Germans.

Melchior also detailed the consequences of these tensions: a libel case that led to prison terms and fines, the trial court’s decision being strengthened on appeal, and the erosion of state support for Jewish writers; through it all, the synagogue faced renewed threats, including an attempted arson in 1942. His testimony is historically significant for capturing the fragile boundary between assimilation and persecution in occupied Denmark, the evolving interactions between Danish institutions and German authority, and the enduring leadership within the Danish Jewish community as it faced a transition from relative safety to peril.
