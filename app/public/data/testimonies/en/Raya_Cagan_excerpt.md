Raya Kagan, a Jewish woman from Vilna who had gone to Paris to pursue a history doctorate, testified about her detention in 1942, her transfer first to the Prefecture and then to Tourelles, where an SS officer she learned of only by rumor was Dannecker who selected young, fit women for deportation. She and sixty-seven women were sent to Drancy and then by cattle train to Auschwitz, where she survived by being chosen for clerical work in the Politische Abteilung and the Standesamt under Untersturmführer Maximilian Grabner, performing administrative duties within the camp’s inner bureaucracy. There she helped run the Registratur and Standesamt— recording births, marriages, and especially deaths— and her testimony exposes the chilling fusion of bureaucratic record-keeping with murder, a central documentary link in the Eichmann trial that shows how ordinary procedures facilitated mass annihilation.
