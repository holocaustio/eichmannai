Walli Malka Zimet, a Prague-based Jewish clerk who worked for HIAS before the German entry and later served in the Zentralstelle fuer juedische Auswanderung within the Community Council, checked and prepared Emigration Kits for those leaving Czechoslovakia. She recalled a chilling sequence through the offices—the surrender of all property and apartments, the obligation to depart with only a few kilograms, and the continual presence of Czech officials, the SS, and the Gestapo, with Eichmann visiting from time to time. Her testimony illuminates the bureaucratic machinery by which emigration was weaponized, revealing Eichmann’s reach into daily administration and providing crucial evidence of how policy and coercion propelled Jews from their homes toward an uncertain, perilous fate.
