Dr. Aharon Peretz, then head of the Gynecology Department at Rambam Hospital in Haifa, had been in Kaunas when the German-Soviet war began and endured forced labor alongside his medical duties. He testified to the early weeks of occupation, recounting the Slobodka pogrom, the mass killings in a garage where Jews were suffocated by hose pipes, and the Seventh Fort massacre in which about 7,000 men were shot after lying on sun-baked sand. He also described the closing of the ghetto on 15 August 1941, the “black Thursday” disappearances of more than a thousand people, and the intelligentsia who were lured out to sort archives only to be shot, a sequence that illustrated the deliberate machinery of extermination and underscored the vital importance of eyewitness testimony for reconstructing the Holocaust in Lithuania.
