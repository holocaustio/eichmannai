Benno Cohen, born in 1894 in Posen, was a Berlin-based lawyer, a veteran of the German Army in World War I, and a lifelong leader in Jewish and Zionist public life who remained on the German bar rolls until 1938, though he practiced law only until 1933. He testified about the Nazi seizure of power, naming the Reichstag fire of February 27, 1933, and the following day’s abolition of civil rights as the birth of an Unrechtsstaat that signaled the rapid erosion of the rule of law. As chairman of the Berlin Zionist Organization and liaison with the Palestine Office, he described the long queues outside Meinecke Street where his colleagues and he worked to aid Jews—a moving portrait of communal resilience as persecution intensified and emigration began to reshape the fate of countless lives.
