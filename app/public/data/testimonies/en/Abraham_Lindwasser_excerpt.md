Avraham Lindwasser, forty-two, an electrician by trade who worked for the Ministry of Defence and lived in Givatayim, testified about arriving at Treblinka from Warsaw on 28 August 1942 after a station notice promised that Jews would be sent “east, to work.” He recalled being ruthlessly forced to alight, stripped of money and jewelry, and then lined up under blows as a German SS officer—“the Hauptmann with the glasses”—selected him and another Jew for separate inspection. In Treblinka II, he was compelled to drag corpses and, when the officer learned he knew something of dentistry, to extract teeth with forceps beside the gas chambers—a brutal task that shattered his disbelief in extermination and, corroborated by the court photograph, stood as stark documentary proof of the camp’s industrialized murder.
