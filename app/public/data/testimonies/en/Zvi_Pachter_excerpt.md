Hirsch Pachter, a Jewish resident of Hrubieszow, testified about being rounded up on 2 December 1939 and forced into a long death march as German SS and Gestapo officers herded men aged 15 to 60, confiscating valuables and leaving them with 20 zloty. He recalled the four-column formation, the orders to remain silent and unmoving, the cries of women and children behind them, and the occasional gunshot as the exhausted laggards were shot, including the heartbreaking moment when a young girl shouted “Daddy” and was abruptly removed near Holotoshins. His account starkly illuminates the early Nazi deportations from Poland, revealing how mass coercion, terror, and property seizure were woven into a brutal prelude to extermination, a testimony of power and suffering that underscored the Eichmann trial’s effort to document systemic atrocity.
