Avraham Karasik, a locksmith by trade who lived in Bialystok and later settled in Rehovot, testified about the German occupation and the fate of the city’s Jewish community. He recounted the mass arrests of thousands, the establishment of a distant ghetto, the emergence of Wehrmacht labor workshops, and the shift to work detachments under armed guards, culminating in the February 1943 roundups that closed the gates and sent many to Treblinka. He also recalled acts of resistance, most notably Yitzchak Malmed’s courageous attack on an SS officer with sulfuric acid, a memory that starkly juxtaposed human courage with the machinery of liquidation and provided a crucial on-the-ground chronicle of how the Bialystok ghetto was dismantled and its people deported to extermination.
