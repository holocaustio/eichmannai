Judge Michael A. Musmanno, a long-serving jurist who sat on the Pennsylvania Supreme Court and, earlier, presided over pivotal war-crimes trials in Nuremberg, testified about his postwar efforts to locate Hitler’s fate. He recalled interviewing more than a hundred Hitler intimates—admirals, generals, aides, secretaries, and even a barber and a cook—who, in the wake of the liberated camps, spoke of Eichmann as a central figure in the extermination program, with Goering admitting he did not know the full scale and with Ribbentrop and Kaltenbrunner naming Eichmann among those most responsible alongside Hitler, Bormann, Himmler and Heydrich. The testimony underscored the painstaking construction of the Nazi network of culpability and the moral urgency of accountability, as Frank and others sought to curb the slaughter while Eichmann’s enduring role stood at the center of the historical record and the postwar reckoning.
