Dr. Mordechai Chen, a physician who directed the anaesthetic services at Tel Aviv Municipal Hospital and who had served as a doctor in the British Army, traveling with a group of doctors to Bergen-Belsen soon after its liberation. He recounted the overwhelming shock of the camp—heaps of unburied bodies, vast pits, huts and tents, and, most moving, emaciated adults whose bodies bore the marks of extreme starvation and who moved with a vacant, almost heeless gaze—and he secured nine photographs from a BBC photographer to preserve what might otherwise be doubted. He insisted that the images be kept for a future reckoning, believing that ten years hence the public and perhaps even the witnesses themselves might need the truth of Bergen-Belsen to confront those responsible, a conviction that anchored the historical record and the moral imperative of postwar accountability.
