Ya'akov Gurfein, a Sanok Jew who later lived in Tel Aviv, testified about the Jews of Sanok and his own experience in the town as the Nazis tightened their control, working in the houses of the German police and remaining with his family in a Gestapo-guarded camp. He recalled the abrupt transformation of Sanok into a ghetto, the Kennkarten registration, and the gathering of Jews into the Zaslav camp, the heart-wrenching separation of children from their mothers, and the deportation of 10,000 people in railway wagons, while 500 who could not be counted or did not report were shot at the edge of a pit, including his uncle. Gurfein’s testimony laid bare the mechanized cruelty of the regime—Judenrat collaboration, Gestapo directives, and the routinized violence that turned bureaucratic procedures into instruments of murder—providing a stark, indispensable eyewitness record central to understanding the scope and mechanics of the Holocaust.
