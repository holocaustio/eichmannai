Moritz Fleischmann, a Vienna-born Jewish communal leader who served on the Vienna City Council and led numerous organizations—from the charitable associations to the Association of Austrian Fur Manufacturers and Merchants, and a Presidium member of the Jewish National Fund—left Vienna for England on 28 August 1939 without a permit. He recalled how the Nazi ascent was greeted with enthusiasm, and how Jewish institutions were abruptly closed and placed under Gestapo and SS control, with their welfare offices blocked from aiding the poor; yet he and a small circle met near the Metropol Hotel, where Gestapo headquarters stood, to clandestinely distribute private relief through figures like Dr. J. H. Koerner, Leopold Foerster, and Emil Engel. He also described a March summons to the Hotel Metropol, where six leaders—Adolf Boehm, Leopold Plaschkes, Leo Goldhammer, Y. H. Kroerner, Alois Rothenberg, and Fleischmann himself—were invited by Gestapo officials, a moment that underscored the rapid destruction of communal life and the emergence of a coercive machinery that would soon drive deportations and genocide.
