Dr. Leon Weliczker Wells, born Leon Weliczker in Stojanow near Lvov and later a prominent American mechanical engineer and scientist, testified as a survivor of Lvov who was sixteen when German and Ukrainian forces arrested him and his father in 1941. He recounted the arrest by Ukrainian police collaborating with the SS, the brutal beatings that claimed lives, and the scene at Pelczynska Street where thousands of Jews were penned together and subjected to a chilling display of cruelty—a white-aproned SS man signaling the next “victim” after a staged scream to terrify the crowd. Wells had kept notes during the war that later became The Death Brigade, about Sonderkommando 1005, and his testimony helped illuminate the machinery of mass murder and the role of local collaborators, providing a crucial eyewitness link in the Eichmann Trial and in the broader history of the Holocaust.
