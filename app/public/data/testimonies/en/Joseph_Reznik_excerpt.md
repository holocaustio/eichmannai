Joseph Reznik, a Tel Aviv shop owner who had served in the Polish army and survived captivity as a German prisoner of war, testified about his forced labor at Majdanek in Lublin and later witnessing its transformation into an extermination site. He recounted the events of 2–3 November 1943, when transports from Lublin and surrounding camps were driven into Majdanek’s fifth field, hundreds of men and hundreds of women were selected and then led to pits beyond the camp to be shot as machine guns rang out, while music blared to mask their approach. Reznik’s account illuminates the calculated efficiency of the killings, the SS-driven selection, and Majdanek’s central role in the systematic murder of Jews, providing crucial eyewitness evidence of the Holocaust’s industrial scale.
