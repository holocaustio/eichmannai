Dr. David Paul Meretz, a Czech Jewish lawyer who had practiced in Maehrisch-Ostrau and, as chairman of the Zionist Organization of Czechoslovakia, led Jewish affairs from Prague after the Nazi takeover, testified to the abrupt collapse of normal life and the need to reorganize under occupation. He described how the Palestine Office in Prague was closed and then reopened within the first week after 15 March under Gestapo supervision, run by a five-member executive—Ya’akov Edelstein as director, Franz Kahn as secretary, with Zucker and Frantisek Friedmann handling administration and finance, while Meretz himself chaired the group—having abolished the committee system to survive. He recalled the perilous options for refugees, including the ten entry permits to England issued by the British Legation that bore the dangerous inscription recognizing their efforts "on behalf of the refugees from Germany," and placed within the broader moment when Jews from the Sudetenland fled into the Czech zone after Munich, illustrating how the regime transformed professional leadership into a precarious, coerced administration amid fear and displacement.
