Alfred Oppenheimer, a Luxembourg Jew who had lived there since 1926, served as a member of the Jewish Consistoire and acted as a liaison between the Gestapo and the community. He recounted how, after the German occupation began in May 1940, Jews were stripped of their belongings, confined in a monastery, and ultimately deported—first to Theresienstadt and then to Auschwitz-Birkenau—where his wife died, and he endured a brutal, days-long journey in a tightly packed train. His testimony documents the systematic destruction of Luxembourg’s Jewish community—674 deported with only 36 surviving—while detailing the disbanding of communal leadership and the complicity of civil authorities, a stark hinge in the larger history of the Holocaust and the fragile refuge Luxembourg briefly offered to many Jews.
