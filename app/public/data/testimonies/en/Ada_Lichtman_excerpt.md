Ada Lichtman, known in Yiddish as Ethel, was a Jewish resident of Wieliczka near Cracow who had been studying and working there before the war began. She recounted the early days of occupation, when German soldiers beat Jewish men, forced them to strip and sweep the streets, and on September 12, 1939, her father and thirty-two others were taken from their homes, photographed, and marched to the marketplace before being loaded onto a truck; she followed and buried her father the next day. Her testimony also described the sealing off of Cracow’s Jewish quarter, brutal raids, and a ritual humiliation of a pious man forced to dance and pray with a hen in a German officer’s presence, illustrating the intimate, pervasive terror that the Eichmann trial sought to document as Nazi violence in occupied Poland.
