Leiona Neumann, a Vienna-born Jewish survivor, testified that she and her mother were seized on 18 January 1942 by Gestapo-backed orderlies, packed into sealed wagons with about 1,500 others, and subjected to a six-day journey to Riga without water or proper food. Upon arrival the SS beat them, allocated the elderly and children to the ghetto, and drove many to death by cold, hunger, or firing squads along the way, with those left behind often never seen again and later learned to have perished in Jungfernhof. In Riga she worked in the Linat Tsedek disinfection unit, processing clothing taken from victims shot at the Ninth Fort, and remembered a Latvian man clutching his daughter’s bullet-ridden coat—a haunting, human emblem of the systematized murder and dispossession that defined the Holocaust in Latvia, and a testimony that illuminates both intimate terror and the scale of the Nazi extermination apparatus.
