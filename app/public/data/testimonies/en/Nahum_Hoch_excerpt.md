Nahum Hoch, born in 1928 in Borsa, Transylvania, was the eldest child of a family deported to Auschwitz in 1944 and later testified at the Eichmann trial about his experiences as a survivor. At Birkenau he was separated from his family; after a failed attempt to obtain an extra ration, he endured a brutal punishment in which the upper part of his body was placed into an oven and he was struck twenty-five times, leaving a lifelong red mark and a difficulty sitting on his right side. Three days before Simhat Torah, Hoch and about ten other Hungarian boys planned a midnight breakout from the quarantine blocks (Blocks 11 and 13), knowing the odds and wielding no weapons, and though they expected failure, the plan went into operation exactly as they had planned, a stark, moving act of resistance that illuminates both the cruelty of Auschwitz and the stubborn humanity of its youngest victims.
