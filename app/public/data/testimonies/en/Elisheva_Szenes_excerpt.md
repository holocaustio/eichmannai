Elisheva Szenes, born in Slovakia and a journalist who worked for a Hungarian newspaper, testified about her escape from the mass deportations and her eventual asylum in Hungary after crossing the border on foot to Budapest. She described the terror of Michalovce in May 1942, when the young women were targeted first and some 3,000 Jews were deported, with postcards afterward claiming they had reached places like Lublin or the Hradok cemetery—an insinuation that many had hardly any bread or sustenance left behind, a brutal prelude to the fates awaiting them. In Budapest, she endured four days of Gestapo detention, during which she and other women were kept without food or drink, stood against a wall for hours, and subjected to naked washing in the corridor in the presence of soldiers, while Hungarian ministers and journalists witnessed, a stark testimony to the complicity of official power in the persecution she and countless others endured and a crucial, human record of the machinery of extermination that the Eichmann Trial sought to reveal.
