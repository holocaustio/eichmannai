Ernst Recht, born in Pilsen and then fifty-nine, was the director of the Haase printing house and of two paper factories in Prague, steering a Jewish enterprise that the Protectorate gradually transformed under trusteeship. He recounted the first acts of dispossession—the seizure of his villa and its assignment to a German administrator, and the Yom Kippur 1939 demand to value and deposit his valuables at banks—workings that stripped Jews of income and status even as he was kept formally employed because he was deemed indispensable to the enterprise. In 1941 he moved into the mechanics of persecution, joining the Jewish Community administratively while secretly continuing to work for Haase, and witnessing the creation of the Trusteeship Office under Guenther and the seizure of valuables by Salo Kramer, a sequence that embodied the systematic destruction of Jewish economic life in Prague and presaged deportations.
