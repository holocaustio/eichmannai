Yehiel Dinur, a Polish-born survivor who published under the name Ka-Tzetnik, testified about his two years in Auschwitz, describing the camp as a planet where people were nameless numbers and life followed laws utterly unlike those of the outside world. He spoke of an oath from fellow inmates that gave him what he called a supernatural strength to endure, and of the two years he spent there as a Musselman, watching prisoners leave him behind in the endless queues. The moment of his collapse in the courtroom underscored the weight of such memory, and his testimony—linked to his later, haunting books—stood as a crucial eyewitness record that compelled the world to confront Auschwitz’s systematic atrocity and the imperative for humanity to awaken.
