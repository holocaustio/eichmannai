Esther Goldstein, a member of Kibbutz Kfar Ruppin, testified about her 1944 deportation from Katzow in Carpatho-Russia (then Hungarian territory) to Auschwitz, traveling with her father, mother, three sisters, a married sister with two children, and her brother; only her sisters and her brother survived. She described how photographs in the album captured a web of ordinary people—neighbors, doctors, shopkeepers, and relatives—whom she identified in Auschwitz after the selection, including herself in the center with a white kerchief, and she recalled the death of one sister’s two children. Goldstein’s account, reinforced by the documentary images of roll-calls, the women’s work, and the railway precinct, underscored the systematic brutality of the deportations and camp life, providing crucial human testimony to the Eichmann Trial about how entire communities were uprooted and erased.
