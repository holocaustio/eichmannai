Rahel Eiga Auerbach, a historian by training who headed the Tel Aviv branch of Yad Vashem’s evidence-collection department, testified about her life in Warsaw before and during the war and her collaboration with Emanuel Ringelblum. She recalled Ringelblum summoning her to organize relief work and, in September 1939, to establish a public kitchen in the premises of the Small Merchants Organization, where the first meals were served within days of Hitler’s entry into Warsaw, a powerful testament to communal solidarity amid catastrophe. Her account links the immediate relief efforts with the later, painstaking archival project that preserved the memories and documents of a besieged community, underscoring how the records she helped safeguard became foundational to Holocaust memory and historical understanding.
