Dr. Adolf Avraham Berman, a psychologist by training, served as director of Centos in Warsaw and headed the Jewish psychological and psychotechnical institutions that organized relief for children in the ghetto. He described how Centos mobilized a vast network—about 100 institutions—to aid roughly 25,000 Jewish children, creating 30 dormitories and orphanages for some 4,000 youths, along with 20 day shelters, 30 children’s and youth clubs, and underground schools that kept education alive despite prohibitions, culminating in moments of communal joy such as a May 5 festival just before the fatal action began on July 22, 1942. His testimony underscored the brutal policy of starvation and epidemics that preyed on the young, while also bearing witness to a remarkable, organized effort to preserve humanity amid annihilation, revealing the moral stakes and immense resilience of the Warsaw Jewish community.
