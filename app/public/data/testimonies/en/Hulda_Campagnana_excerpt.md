Hulda Campagnano, born Cassuto, was an Italian Jewish mathematician who taught in Milan and Florence and belonged to a family of scholars, including her father, Professor Cassuto. She testified that, until September 1943, there was no danger to her life—only economic restrictions under the anti-Jewish laws—and that she lived with her husband and two small children in Florence, adapting to a changed world. When the Germans occupied Italy, they took the municipal list of Jews and, house by house, rounded them up to be sent to the Fossoli di Carpi transit camp near Modena, a drastic turning point that exposed the fragility of civilian life under occupation and underscored the systematic deportations at the heart of the Eichmann trial.
