Dr. Ernst Abeles, born in Slovakia and trained as a lawyer, headed the emigration and social welfare departments of the Jewish Central Office, coordinating relief and evacuation for Jewish communities. He described the 1938 episode in Burgenland, where SS forces seized seven religious communities, forced families onto a Danube sandbank with little more than the bare minimum, and left them for six months to endure hunger, rats, and exposure before they were ferried to safety on an abandoned tug. The account showed how quickly civil rights collapsed into brutal anti-Semitic acts and illustrated the precarious work of Jewish relief organizations to save lives, making Abeles’s testimony a stark, indispensable record of Slovakia’s slide from equality to persecution.
