Aharon Hoter-Yishai, then an officer in the Jewish Brigade within Montgomery's Eighth Army, was tasked with coordinating the unit's efforts to locate Jewish relatives and provide aid to survivors across postwar Europe. He testified that he visited dozens of camps, from Dachau to Bergen-Belsen, Mauthausen, and Theresienstadt, and described the Bergen-Belsen toll—about 52,000 refugees present and 27,000 dying during medical treatment after liberation. His stark portrayal of survivors as living beings wounded in body and spirit, the brigade's sudden shift from formal search to wide-ranging relief, and the record of post-liberation suffering illuminated the Nazi genocide's scale and the urgent humanitarian response that followed.
