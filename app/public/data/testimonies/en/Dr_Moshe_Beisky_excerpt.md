Dr. Moshe Beisky, a Tel Aviv magistrate and Polish Jew born in 1921, testified about his wartime experience in Cracow, noting that he avoided the ghetto and moved with his parents to the town of Dzialoszyce, where he remained until a few days before Rosh Hashanah in 1942. He recalled the regime of armbands, curfews, and colossal forced contributions imposed through the Judenrat, as well as the relocation of Jews to labor camps near Cracow, including the Strauch firm and facilities at Kostize, Kobierzynska Street, and even an airport unit. The most harrowing moment came in Miechowitz, when SS men and what appeared to be the Sonderkommando surrounded the village, forced men to perform gymnastics in the central square, and then opened fire, a massacre Beisky witnessed with his own eyes; his testimony captures the local machinery of genocide—the bureaucratic and daily terror that erased a community and exposed the organized reach of Nazi atrocity.
