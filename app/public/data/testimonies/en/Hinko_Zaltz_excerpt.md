Dr. Hinko Salz, a Herzegovinian-born physician who settled in Tel Aviv and had trained as a general practitioner before specializing in dental medicine, served as an army medical lieutenant when World War II began for Yugoslavia and was captured at the Romanian-Yugoslav border. After escaping from a POW camp, he found himself at the Belgrade Kalemajdan fortress, where, though surrounded by thousands of fellow prisoners, he established himself in a fledgling clinic and, using his fluency in German, won the trust of a German medical officer and arranged sustenance through the American Red Cross for those left without food. He then worked as a liaison between the Yugoslav Medical Corps and German headquarters, sorting medical matters and helping to sustain a fragile network of care under occupation, a testament to the difficult moral choices faced by medical professionals during the Holocaust and to the ways human resilience persisted even within the machinery of persecution.
