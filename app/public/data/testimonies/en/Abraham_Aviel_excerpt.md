Abraham Aviel, born in 1927 in Dowgaliszuk near Radun, was a Jewish clerical worker who survived the Radun ghetto and the murderous operations of the Einsatzgruppen. He testified to the December 1941 massacre, when German troops searched the town, forced about forty Jewish refugees to a hill outside Radun, and ordered them buried in the frozen ground—a sight he described as the first time he witnessed that scale of bloodshed—and to his father being taken with that group, and his failed attempt to smuggle his younger brother Yekutiel, who was dressed in shepherd’s clothing to pass as a farmer’s child, out of the ghetto before the boy was recaptured. His account, including the ghetto’s abrupt closure and the perilous searches for family amid constant threat, underscored the intimate human costs of Nazi murder and provided a crucial, humanizing record for the Eichmann Trial’s documentation of the Holocaust.
